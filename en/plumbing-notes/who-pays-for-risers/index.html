Who pays for riser replacement - the undivided share, not the flat count

Who pays for riser replacement in a block of flats? All the flat owners do, each in the amount of their undivided share.

A sworn advocate explains on LV portāls (2024) that the costs, including a loan, are split by undivided share, so by the area of the property, not by the number of flats.

Area, not the number of flats

Article 5, part 1 of the Apartment Property Law defines the undivided share. It is the ratio of the total area of a separate property to the total area of all separate properties in the house. Put simply, the larger a flat is compared with all the flats together, the larger its share of the common property and of shared expenses.

So in the same house a larger flat pays more for riser replacement than a small one, even if the riser passes through both alike. Equally, a flat whose riser does not leak pays no less just because it has had no problems.

Sources: Article 5, part 1 of the Apartment Property Law and a sworn advocate’s reply on LV portāls (2024), viewed on 25 November 2026.

Votes go by flat, payments by area

Votes and payments are counted differently. Article 16, part 3 of the Apartment Property Law says a decision of the community is adopted and binds every owner if a majority voted “for”. That means owners holding more than half of all the flat properties in the house, unless the law or the community’s own rules provide otherwise. Each owner has as many votes as the flat properties they own (Article 17, part 1).

So the owners of a small and a large flat have one vote each, yet pay differently for the risers. If the community has decided to save money for works in advance, Article 13, part 2.1 provides that owners also pay into the savings fund by undivided share. When taking such a decision, the community assesses the documents justifying the works and their cost.

A loan or works contract in the community's name

Large works, such as replacing all risers, can be organised by a community decision, a contract with a builder and sometimes a loan too. The Apartment Property Law states that a flat owner must provide financing, in line with their undivided share, to meet the community’s obligations towards third parties.

This means each owner’s part of the contract or loan obligations equals their undivided share, and the LV portāls explanation splits a loan in exactly this way. If an owner does not pay their part, that is a matter for the building manager and the community, not for a plumber.

A riser passing through a private cellar room

An LV portāls e-consultation (2026) covers risers and basement water mains that serve more than one flat. They are usually common property even if they pass through someone’s private cellar room, and all owners cover their replacement by undivided shares. A branch serving only that room is usually paid for by its owner.

It is similar in a flat: the riser section between floors is usually part of the house, even if hidden in your bathroom boxing. An LV portāls e-consultation (2017), citing Article 10, part 1, point 7 of the Apartment Property Law, explains that an owner must allow the house riser between floors to be replaced. This applies especially if emergencies arise and a meeting of owners has decided it.

The price list figure is not your share

The price list counts riser work per flat height. A section of water riser starts from €110, one on the waste side from €100; materials and the manager’s approval are not included. That is the plumber’s labour price for one section, not the sum you pay.

When a house replaces all risers, an estimate is drawn up for the whole building, and your payment is calculated from it by undivided share. So two neighbours on one riser may pay different sums, and that is correct.

About shares and loans

A riser runs through my kitchen but has never leaked. Must I pay as well?

According to the LV portāls explanation, costs are split by undivided share, not by which flat had damage. A riser is a shared system of the house, and all owners cover the cost of replacing it.

Is loan interest also split by area?

The explanation mentions the loan together with other costs: it too is split by undivided share. How exactly interest is divided in your house’s contract can be seen in the community decision.

A neighbour refuses to pay their part. Do the others pay then?

The law obliges each owner to provide financing to the extent of their share. Article 15, part 2 of the Apartment Property Law says the community answers for obligations with its property. If owners settle with a creditor directly, however, those who have not paid answer first for the community’s obligations, by their undivided shares. How to recover the unpaid sum is a matter for the manager and the community.

I am buying a flat where risers were replaced and are still being paid off. Do I take over the former owner's debt?

Article 13.1, part 2 of the Apartment Property Law says that on voluntary transfer the buyer answers for certain debts that arose no earlier than three years before the transfer. These cover management costs, utility services, the savings fund and land use fees. So before buying, ask the manager for a statement of debts.
