Every price applies to one unit: one fixture, one metre, a riser section one flat high or one hour. Fixtures, parts and materials are not included in these sums.

Per fixture, per metre, per floor or per hour

In the list below, each job has a starting sum beside it. To compare it with your situation, it matters to know what it is counted for:

Per fixture
tap, toilet, sink, bath, shower cabin, water heater, radiator and heated towel rail. The price is for work on that fixture; the fixture itself is extra.
Per metre
fixing new water pipe in an exposed run, from €10/m. The pipe itself, fittings, wall chases and finishing are separate.
Per outlet or connection
a new water or waste outlet at a fixture and replacing the lying drain pipe from the stack to one fixture.
Per floor
a water or sewer riser section one flat high, joined to the old pipe above and below.
Per hour
a list of small jobs and drain washing with a high-pressure jetter; for the yard sewer the price is for the first hour.
Per visit
defect inspection, an urgent case where water must be stopped, and clearing a blockage from the drains in your home.

About the units in the price list

What does an exposed run mean in the per-metre price?

The pipe is fixed on the wall or ceiling surface rather than set into a chase. If it is hidden in a wall, chasing and finishing are charged separately, and the sum also depends on the run length, bends and pipe type.

Does the yard sewer hourly price also apply to a pipe under the house floor?

No. That price covers the line outside the house up to the manhole. Drains inside a flat or house have a separate job, “Clearing a blockage from the drains in your home”, while deposits on pipe walls are washed off with high pressure.

Does the hourly rate suit a large job too?

The hourly rate is meant for a list of small tasks done one after another in a single visit. Large jobs, such as a riser section or a water heater, have their own entry in the list with its own starting price.
