Plumbing work in Pļavnieki - a flat in a tall building

In a high-rise flat, plumbing work in Pļavnieki has to be planned knowing that water and sewer risers are shared by the whole vertical line and the walls are usually concrete.

Tall blocks of flats dominate the neighbourhood, so almost every job first needs to know where the pipes run in the flat, which valve shuts them off and whether the wall may be drilled.

Improved 602 series: where the pipes run in the flat

A Wikipedia overview of standard residential building types says the most widespread type in Pļavnieki is the improved 602 series, box-shaped houses with recessed loggias. The history section of the neighbourhoods portal says construction began in 1985. The neighbourhood also has 467 series blocks and 104 series towers. Before changing a toilet, sink or dishwasher, check a few places that will set the scope of work more than the fixture itself. If the shaft panel was once tiled over, access to it must be solved first, because without it you can neither shut off water nor change a sewer section.

Riser shaft
usually behind the toilet panel or a hatch, holding both the water and sewer risers.
Stop valves
on the branch at the riser; they decide whether water can be shut off in the flat alone during work.
Height of the sewer branch
it decides which toilet or sink fits the old position without alteration.
Kitchen supply pipes
often along the wall or under the worktop; a dishwasher needs a free valve and a waste connection at the trap.
Washing machine position
in the bathroom or kitchen, with the waste branch from it to the riser needing a fall.

Monolithic reinforced concrete towers on Lubānas, Vācieša and Saharova iela

The same overview says Pļavnieki has four monolithic reinforced concrete high-rises on Lubānas, J. Vācieša and A. Saharova iela, counted as the so-called French project. A monolithic wall is one piece of concrete with reinforcement, and that changes plumbing work. A pipe chase cannot be cut in it as in brick, and a drill may hit a bar, so pipes in these flats may run on the surface or in plasterboard boxing. Sink or frame fixings use concrete anchors, and the drilling spot is chosen knowing where cables and pipes may be in the wall. If a new fixture must move further from the shaft, pipes are usually led along the wall and then covered by boxing or furniture.

A damaged pipe: building side or flat side

Cabinet Regulation No. 907 (28 September 2010, as amended), sub-point 17.10: unscheduled repair of a residential house is usually carried out if damage is found in pipelines or their joints with fittings, valves and appliances. Source: likumi.lv, viewed on 25 November 2026.

In a tall building damage to a riser or its joint affects not just one flat but the whole vertical line. If a seep is before the flat’s stop valves, for instance at a riser socket in the shaft, first inform the building manager, who decides on unscheduled repair. If the damage is beyond the valves on the flat branch, it can usually be fixed in the flat. When the manager replaces a riser for the whole building, an owner benefits if the shaft is accessible with no walled-in branches. A soil stack section within the flat can also be changed on its own, but agree it with the manager and upstairs neighbours, who must not use the drain during the work.

The former Hausmaņa purvs bog by Deglava iela

The geography section of the neighbourhoods portal says the north-western part by A. Deglava iela lies on the former Hausmaņa purvs bog, with peat deposits 3–8 m thick and groundwater at 1.5–3 m. Peat compresses over time, so pipes buried on such a base can settle unevenly. The load tends to be greatest where a pipe leaves the building, passing from a rigid structure into the ground. This changes little inside a flat, but if water stands in a basement drain or yard manhole, the cause may be a settled pipe, not just a blockage. Water in a cellar or manhole does not always come from the sewer, so find its source before cleaning.

Pipe shaft, concrete wall and banging pipes

There is a sound of running water in the riser shaft, but nothing is wet. Whom to tell?

In a tall building water runs in the water riser almost all the time, as many flats use it, so the sound alone is no fault. If it does not stop at night, some flat’s cistern may be running constantly or the riser may be seeping. First check your own cistern. If it is fine, report the sound to the building manager, who can inspect the shaft on other floors. A sound that grows when someone upstairs uses water is normal, but a steady gurgle all night is a sign that water is flowing somewhere without pause.

A sink has to hang on a monolithic wall. Can it be drilled anywhere?

No. Pipes and cables usually run in the wall above and below existing taps and sockets, so do not drill there. In monolithic concrete you may hit a bar; then move the hole a few centimetres instead of drilling through the reinforcement. A heavy sink or frame needs anchors made for concrete, and the hole is cleared of dust so the anchor holds. For a heavy sink or one with a cabinet, four fixing points are better than two, and remember that neighbours' pipes may also run near the wall.

Pipes bang when a tap is closed. What to check in the flat?

The bang usually comes from water stopped sharply: a single-lever mixer closed in one move, or an appliance’s electric valve. In tall buildings pressure on lower floors is higher, so the bang may be louder. In the flat, check that pipes are firmly fixed, since a loose pipe moves and knocks. If the bang is strong, a pressure reducer after the stop valves may help. A short flexible hose on the appliance supply can also absorb part of it.
