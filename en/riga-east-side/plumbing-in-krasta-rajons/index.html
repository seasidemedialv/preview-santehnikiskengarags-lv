Plumber in Krasta rajons - panel towers and retail buildings

In a tower with a single stairwell, a plumber in Krasta rajons works with risers that link many flats in one vertical line.

Before changing a tap, toilet or towel rail, find out whether water can be shut off in the flat itself or the whole line has to be stopped. Beside the homes along Krasta iela stand shops and offices, and their plumbing too is connected to building networks.

One vertical line through the whole tower

A Wikipedia overview of standard residential building types says that 104 series panel towers with one stairwell are common in the Krasta iela estate. In such a building each water and sewer riser passes through every floor and serves flats stacked one above another. If a riser has to be shut off, usually the whole vertical line loses water, not just one flat, and the riser is refilled after the work. So in towers it matters a great deal that flat repairs can be done without touching the shared pipe.

Stop valves right after the riser branch in the flat make this possible. If they exist and hold water, work on a tap, toilet or washing machine can be done without isolating the riser. If there are none, or they are so old they no longer close, the building manager usually shuts the riser, and it then makes sense to replace the stop valves at the same time. Afterwards, close and open the new valve a few times so it does not seize, and keep it within reach, not tiled into boxing.

Large-panel blocks and newer flats by “Lido”

The Krasta estate also has 467 series large-panel blocks, built in Riga in the 1970s and 1980s. In them pipes may run exposed or in the toilet boxing, where they can be reached without major dismantling. Newer flats can differ. In January 2019 TVNET reported that construction of the “Krasta kvartāls” flats, with six planned buildings, had begun by the “Lido” leisure centre at what was then Maskavas iela 192. In new buildings water pipes may be hidden in walls and floor, and the toilet hung on a concealed frame. There you get at the cistern via the flush plate opening or a service hatch, and a fault may not show outside until water appears on the floor or ceiling.

Low ground on the Daugava valley side

Relief
between the Daugava and Latgales iela, formerly Maskavas iela, the land is mostly flat and low, 3–4 m above sea level.
Ground
in the built-up part of the Daugava valley the top layer is man-made deposits, with loose sand of varying grain size below containing silt layers whose thickness ranges from 1–3 m to 6–8 m.

The neighbourhoods portal gives both facts for the whole Latgale neighbourhood, not for each house, yet they matter in pipework. In fill and loose ground, pipes under the yard and building entry can settle unevenly over time. Movement may show first as a crack or loose joint where the line leaves the building. On a flat, low site the yard sewer may also have little fall to spare. After independence was restored, intensive construction of retail and office buildings began in this part. Near retail buildings the yard pipes may lie under asphalt or paving, and then every excavation also means restoring the surface.

No water in the whole building

If it is not only your taps but every flat that has no water, the first question is whether the city water main is damaged. You can find out from the building manager or the notices of “Rīgas ūdens”. A failure in the city network is repaired by the water service provider, not a plumber, and there is nothing to fix in the flat. If instead the neighbours have water and only you do not, the fault is most likely in the flat or riser branch. Examples are a closed or broken stop valve, a blocked supply pipe or a damaged joint. Then you can request an urgent visit, but if water is running, stop it with the nearest valve.

Riga City Council binding regulations No. 17 (15 December 2017), point 55: this applies when a city network failure interrupts water supply. The water service provider must then usually restore it within 48 hours at the latest; in complex cases the period may be extended. This is the provider’s deadline, not a plumber’s working time. Source: likumi.lv, viewed on 25 November 2026.

Shop, hidden pipes and rust

A sink is blocked in a ground floor shop or office with flats above. Could the fault be in the shared drain?

It could. If the premises' waste is joined to the building sewer below them, a blockage in the house’s lying drain usually shows first at the lowest point, so right on the ground floor. If water rises in other fixtures or in the cellar too, the problem is probably in the shared pipe, and the manager should be told. If only one sink drains slowly, the blockage is most likely in its trap or the short branch to the shared pipe. In cafés and catering premises fat gets into the drain and hardens in the pipe. Pressure jetting can wash it out, but a drain snake alone may not be enough.

The laminate is swelling near a drain but the pipes are hidden. Where should you start looking?

With the nearest fixtures: trap joints under the sink, washing machine or dishwasher hoses, the bath outlet and the concealed cistern frame. If the floor gets wetter only while an appliance runs, the fault is usually in the waste. If damp grows at night too, when nobody uses water, the cause may be a pressurised water pipe. Open the wall or floor only after the nearest fixtures and their joints have been checked without finding the fault, and then over as small an area as possible.

Rust has appeared on the towel rail connections. Is that already a leak?

Not always. A dry, unchanging layer of rust may be the trace of an old seep that has since stopped. If the rust is wet, growing or leaves marks on the floor below, the joint is seeping and rust will eat through it in time. In towers a rail may be connected directly to the hot riser, so replacing it may require the riser to be shut off. Plan that with the manager, not when the joint is already leaking.
