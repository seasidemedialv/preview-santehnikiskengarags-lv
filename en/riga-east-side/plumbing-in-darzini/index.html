Plumber in Dārziņi - heat and hot water in your own house

A plumber in Dārziņi works on houses where radiators, heating circuit and hot water form the building’s private system, as the neighbourhood has no district heating.

To change one radiator or hang a water heater you need not wait for a heat supplier to shut off a riser, but draining, refilling and pressure of the circuit are the house owner’s responsibility. Water and waste water are often the owners' own business here too.

A heating circuit of your own, without city heat

The Dārziņi plan (2017), describing the existing situation, says the neighbourhood has no district heating and is outside the AS “Rīgas siltums” supply zone. In a 2013 residents' survey most used firewood for heating. So heat here is usually the owner’s own affair: a private heat source and a private radiator circuit. For the plumber, everything happens inside the house: there is usually no shared riser to settle with neighbours, but refilling and bleeding afterwards also fall to the household.

Changing a radiator in such a house begins with the question of how to isolate the circuit. If the radiator has two valves that hold the water, it can be replaced without draining the circuit. With no valves, water is drawn off at least to radiator level; after the work the system is refilled, bled and its pressure restored. While the circuit is empty, the boiler must not be fired. When hanging a water heater, what matters most is a wall that holds the full tank and somewhere to lead the safety valve water. If hot water has not been heated in a tank here before, you also need to find where the cold supply can reach the heater and how to take hot water to the taps.

Borehole, well and a private waste water system

The same plan document holds a 2012 residents' survey: two thirds took water from local boreholes up to 20 m deep and one third from wells. For waste water, a third had cesspits and the rest a biological treatment unit on the plot. The survey is old and things may have changed in many places since, but it shows well what indoor plumbing here is connected to. Most plots with detached houses are 400-1200 m², the majority under 600 m², so there is not much room for pipes in the yard.

Water from a borehole or well
the house usually has a pump and a pressure tank, and the tap pressure is what the pump is set to. Take this into account when fitting a new tap, shower or water heater, because some appliances need steady pressure. If the pump stutters after a tap is opened, running briefly and stopping, the fault may lie in the pressure tank, not the new tap.
Cesspit
every litre from the tap ends up in the pit, so a leaking toilet or dripping tap costs not only water but also more frequent emptying. Such a leak is worth stopping at once.
Private treatment unit
the waste pipe from house to unit needs an even fall, and the house drain must not receive what has no place there, for example rainwater from the roof.
Small plot
little room remains between house, borehole, pit and the neighbour’s boundary, so pipes often run close to walls or under paths. Plan a new branch or supply inlet so that it can be dug up later.

Downpours, a pond and filled-in ditches

Riga City Council binding regulations No. 17 (15 December 2017), point 63.9: groundwater and surface water must not be discharged into the sewer without agreement with “Rīgas ūdens”. Source: likumi.lv, viewed on 25 November 2026.

The 2017 plan account mentions a pond with no outflow. It takes rainwater from a warehouse area at then Maskavas iela, now Latgales iela, 464, and in heavy downpours floods nearby plots by overflowing. Several drainage ditches there are described as overgrown, silted or filled in. For a house this means rainwater must find its own path, not the house drain. If water gets into the cellar or the drain gully after rain, the temptation to send it into the sewer is understandable. It must not go into the city sewer without agreement with “Rīgas ūdens”, and in your own pit or unit it can overload the system. Give roof and yard water its own route into the ground or to a ditch that is not filled in.

Your own heating circuit and hot water tank

One radiator has to come off the house heating circuit. Must the whole system be drained?

Not always. If the radiator has valves on both flow and return pipes and they hold the water, both are closed and only the radiator itself is emptied. With no valves, or valves that do not hold, the circuit is drained at least to the bottom of the radiator. If the circuit holds frost-proof fluid, it is collected in a container, not poured down the drain. After the work the circuit is refilled, bled and checked to make sure the pressure does not fall. If all of it has to be drained after all, it is a good moment to change other valves that no longer close, as the system will not need emptying a second time.

Can one hot water tank be warmed by the heating in winter and by electricity in summer?

Yes, such tanks have both a heat exchanger for heating water and an electric element. In winter, with the boiler lit, the heating circuit warms the water; in summer the element is switched on. The tank is connected to both the water supply and the heating circuit, and valves are needed on the heating side so that it can be separated from the boiler in summer. The electrical connection is done by an electrician. Such a tank is usually larger and heavier than a plain electric one, so it needs a solid wall or a floor-standing position.

The water heater has to go in an unheated utility room. What should be considered?

First, frost: if the room drops below zero in winter, a full tank and its pipes may burst, so they must be drainable. Second, heat loss, as a cold room keeps cooling the tank. Third, safety valve water needs a drain that does not freeze itself. If possible, the heater is better placed in a heated room near the taps. A tank full of water is very heavy, so a light partition needs extra support or another spot must be found.
