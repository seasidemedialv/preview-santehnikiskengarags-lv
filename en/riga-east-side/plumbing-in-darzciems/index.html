Plumber in Dārzciems - rainwater that runs through the house

In a flat-roofed block a plumber in Dārzciems meets pipes that carry not waste water from the flats but rainwater from the roof: 103 series buildings drain it through an internal system.

So a damp patch by the pipe shaft after a downpour may come from the roof drain, not from the soil stack. Beside these blocks the neighbourhood also has small family houses with plumbing of their own, quite different.

A roof water pipe inside the building

A Wikipedia overview of standard residential building types describes 103 series blocks as having a basement and a flat roof drained by an internal system, and this series occurs in Dārzciems. It means one more vertical pipe runs through the building from roof to basement, neither water supply nor domestic sewer. Rain and snowmelt flow through it, and it belongs to the whole building, like the roof. If it gets blocked somewhere with leaves or sand, water may collect on the roof and look for another way.

If damp appears by the shaft or on a ceiling, the first question is timing. A seep from the rainwater pipe usually shows after rain or a thaw and dries out in dry weather. A seep from the soil stack is tied to someone upstairs using water, and may come with a smell. A seep from a water pipe usually runs whatever the weather and use. The first two cases are usually dealt with by the building as a whole, so the manager should hear of them first. In the flat, a seep on your own branch beyond the stack can be fixed.

Family houses and later blocks side by side

Small houses around Sesku iela and Kārsavas iela

The neighbourhoods portal’s geography section places detached housing around the side streets of Dārzciema iela, such as Sesku iela and Kārsavas iela, and along Ilūkstes iela. The history section says many single-family houses were built here after the Second World War. A common type was the one-storey “inkwell” house with a hipped roof and a central chimney. In such a house the central chimney takes up the middle, so pipes and the water heater may be placed along outer walls or in a kitchen corner. Yard pipes may lie shallower than the groundwater, which in the neighbourhood is mostly at a depth of 1.5–3 m. In old houses water and sewer pipes may have been added later than the house was built, and pipes may run along the floor.

Multi-storey blocks around Vestienas iela and Pildas iela

According to the portal, multi-storey blocks stand in the north around Vestienas, Nīcgales and Vietalvas iela and in the east around Pildas, Strautu and Rēzeknes iela. Wikipedia says that in the 1970s some single-family houses were demolished and blocks of flats built in their place. In places a family house and a block may stand very close, each with its own supply connection and sewer. In a block, work in a flat depends on the risers and the building manager; in a family house it is all up to the owner, right to the yard manhole.

City sewer connection and the application deadline

LV portāls writes that in 2025 Dārzciems owners whose properties already have a central sewer nearby also received municipal co-funding for a sewer connection. For a house joined to the city network, the inside may change too: the old outlet position, the transition from the house pipe to the new connection and sometimes the whole ground floor drain. If the connection is built or being built, it is worth knowing the deadline for applying for co-funding.

Riga City Council binding regulations No. RD-23-207-sn (28 June 2023, as amended), point 7: the co-funding application is submitted no later than three years after completion of the construction works. Source: likumi.lv, viewed on 25 November 2026.

This means the period is counted from the end of the works, so keep the completion documents and invoices together. If the drain starts running slowly after the house is connected, the cause may be the old pipe joined to the new one, not the connection itself. Deposits may have built up over decades in an old cast iron or clay pipe, and they remain after the house outlet is linked to the new connection. Then first clear the blockage and afterwards decide whether to replace the old section.

Rainwater shaft, chimney and shower

In a 103 series house a damp strip appears by the inner shaft after rain. Whom to tell first, the manager or a plumber?

The manager first. If damp appears right after rain, most likely the roof water pipe or its joint is leaking; it is a system of the whole building, for which the building usually answers. The manager can also arrange a shaft inspection on other floors to find where the water starts. If the damp is there in dry weather too, a sewer or water riser may be damaged, but those are usually common property as well.

In a small house the water heater has to go beside the central chimney. Is that spot suitable?

What matters most is whether the wall holds the weight of a full tank, because fixings may not hold in old masonry. If there is a solid wall nearby with a short route to the taps, it is usually the better choice. If there is no other place, first find out whether anything may be fixed to the chimney masonry at all; a chimney sweep can judge that. Moving the old heater is also a good moment to drain and descale it, while it is off the wall anyway.

I want a shower cubicle where there is no drain in the floor. Can it be joined to the sink waste?

Sometimes, if the sink waste pipe is low enough and the cubicle tray high enough to give the water a fall. A sink branch is often narrower and may not carry shower and sink water away well at once. It is safer to run a separate branch to the stack or house drain. Choose the tray height and outlet position before buying the cubicle.
