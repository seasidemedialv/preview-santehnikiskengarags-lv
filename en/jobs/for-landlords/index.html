Plumber for landlords - what the tenancy law says about damage

A plumber for landlords is needed when something leaks or water stands in a drain in a rented flat while the owner lives elsewhere.

Under the Law on Residential Tenancy, the tenant informs the landlord of such damage immediately. The landlord, as far as it depends on them, must maintain the building so the premises can be lived in. A defect inspection in Ķengarags costs from €40.

The tenant's notice and the landlord's duty

Article 17, part 1, point 7 of the Law requires the tenant to inform the landlord immediately of damage or defects that affect or may affect a person’s health, life or property. A leaking joint under the sink or an overflowing drain can damage both the flat’s own floor and the neighbour’s ceiling below, so these are usually exactly that kind of damage.

Article 18, part 3 of the Law in turn states the landlord’s duty. As far as it depends on them, they must maintain the residential building and run it in line with legislation, so that the tenant can live in the premises. In practice, the landlord needs to know about a dripping tap or a clogged drain and decide what happens next.

This duty has consequences. Suppose the landlord fails to meet the duties set out in the first or third part of Article 18. Under Article 27, part 2, the tenant may then withdraw from the agreement immediately, with written notice to the landlord. Unrepaired damage that gets in the way of living there can therefore cost more than the repair itself.

Law on Residential Tenancy, Article 17, part 1, point 7, Article 18, part 3 and Article 27, part 2 (likumi.lv, checked 25 November 2026).

When the tenant wants to change a tap

The tenant has a limit too: under Article 17, part 1, point 5, they may not carry out unauthorised alterations or unauthorised repair works in the residential premises. This usually covers plumbing as well, such as a new mixer tap, a different toilet or a dishwasher connection, if the landlord has not agreed to it.

Article 8, part 2 of the Law allows the tenancy agreement to include terms on carrying out improvements, including repair works, in the premises. The agreement can state which small plumbing jobs the tenant may order alone and which need your approval first. Then there is no later argument about whether a job was permitted.

The agreement can say, for example, that the tenant may request an urgent visit alone if water is running and the landlord cannot be reached. Replacing a tap, toilet or water heater and connecting new fixtures would need the landlord’s approval first. The tenant then knows what to do at night, and you know what you will be paying for.

A worn tap or a damaged tap

Article 2150 of the Civil Law states that a tenant must use the thing properly and prudently, and if they have done so, they are not liable for ordinary wear. The Civil Law applies to residential tenancy insofar as the Law on Residential Tenancy does not regulate the matter. So a tap whose cartridge has worn out over the years is not the same as a tap the tenant has broken.

An LV portāls online consultation (2026) stresses what matters in a dispute over damage. One thing is whether the condition of the premises was recorded in a handover report when the tenancy began, and another is how the damage can be repaired. In such a dispute, a plumber’s view of what has broken and how to repair it may be useful alongside the report.

If the tenant has paid for a necessary repair, Article 2140 of the Civil Law provides that the landlord reimburses, under the general rules, necessary and useful expenses the tenant has put into the thing. That does not mean every job a tenant orders without permission must be repaid. Conversely, a tenant may damage the premises, their fittings or the building’s pipework, or break the agreed rules on repair works. Article 22, point 1 of the tenancy law then lets the landlord end the agreement in the manner set by law.

Landlord and tenant

The tenant has already changed the mixer tap. Was that allowed?

The law forbids a tenant to carry out unauthorised repair works. Whether the tap change was permitted usually depends on the tenancy agreement and on whether you consented. If the agreement says nothing about it, you can write it into the next version.

The agreement says nothing about plumbing. What happens if the toilet leaks?

The tenant must tell you, and you as landlord, as far as it depends on you, must maintain the building so it can be lived in. The plumber can then be requested by the landlord or, with the landlord’s consent, by the tenant.

The tenant mentioned a damp patch only a month later. Does the law require telling at once?

Yes. For damage that affects or may affect health, life or property, the law requires immediate notice. A damp patch from a leaking pipe can damage property, so it is worth repeating this rule in the agreement.
