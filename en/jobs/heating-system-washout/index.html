Heating system flushing - writing down the valve settings before the work

Heating system flushing has one side effect: if the valve settings get lost, the radiators may heat unevenly afterwards.

A house or flat with its own heating circuit needs flushing when deposits have built up in the heating water and some radiators no longer warm up. The price depends on the number of radiators, how dirty the water is and whether a flushing agent is used. For that reason this job has no single starting price.

Valve positions noted before the flush

Kamco, a maker of flushing machines, advises in its Clearflow instructions that every radiator valve setting is recorded before flushing. That means the number of turns each valve is open, counted from closed. After the work the system can then easily be set as it was, without balancing it again.

Balancing is the adjustment after which the farthest and nearest radiators each receive their share of heat. If someone has done it in the past, that work is valuable and should not be lost in a flush. The record can be simple: room, radiator, valve and number of turns.

Room Radiator Valve Turns from closed
Bedroom by the window thermostatic write the number
Kitchen on the wall return write the number

This is only a sample record. Count the turns by carefully closing the valve fully and then opening it back to where it was. If the valve has a presetting mark, go by that instead.

Presettings opened all the way

The manufacturer Purmo states for its panel radiators that if the pipework needs flushing, the presettings of the radiator valves are opened to maximum. They are adjusted only after the flush. A fully open valve lets more water through, so deposits are washed out of the radiator more easily.

The two instructions complement each other. First the position of every valve is noted, then the presettings are opened, the system is flushed and the valves are returned to the recorded positions.

Kamco Clearflow instructions and the Purmo panel radiator instructions, checked on 25 November 2026. These are manufacturers' guidance; for a particular system, see the documents for its radiators and valves.

The worst radiator goes first

Kamco gives one more rule of order: if the first inspection finds cold or partly clogged radiators, flushing radiators one by one starts with the worst. The scope of work and the price are affected by:

Number of radiators
the more there are on the circuit, the longer the work takes.
State of the heating water
dark, cloudy water with particles needs more flushing than nearly clean water.
Flushing agent
if one is used, it is a separate material and affects the price.

Flow direction, a reading and what stays in the system

In the Kamco flushing procedure the thermostatic valves are opened fully, their heads are removed and each valve pin is checked for free movement. Each radiator is then flushed on its own, reversing the flow regularly, until the cold spots disappear and the temperature is even across the whole radiator.

Kamco advises judging when to stop by a reading rather than by eye. The total dissolved solids (TDS) of the outflowing water are compared with tap water. Flushing continues until the two readings differ by no more than 5-10%. After that the system is full of clean water. Kamco states that a good-quality corrosion inhibitor has to be added to it so that new deposits do not form.

The radiator material must be known before the work. Kamco does not allow its acidic agent POWERFLUSH FX2 in systems with aluminium radiators or heat exchangers; an acid-free agent is meant for those. A specialist applies the flushing agent and inhibitor following the manufacturer’s directions.

Kamco Clearflow instructions, viewed on 25 November 2026. This is the procedure of a flushing equipment manufacturer, not a Latvian standard.

A block of flats and a house with its own boiler

In a block of flats on district heating the heating system is shared. Its flushing usually has to be discussed with the building manager, unless the house documents say otherwise. In a private house with its own boiler and heating circuit the owner decides alone, and the valve record stays with the owner for next time too.

Flushing and valve adjustment

What happens if the valve positions were not noted before flushing?

The valves then have to be set from scratch afterwards, and the system may need balancing again so that all radiators heat evenly. That is why Kamco advises making the record before the work.

Why are the valve presettings opened fully during a flush?

Purmo states that this is to be done when the pipework is flushed, and adjusting starts only afterwards. An open valve gives more flow, so deposits are easier to wash out.

How is the worst radiator found?

The first inspection looks for radiators that are cold or warm only in part. Kamco advises starting the radiator-by-radiator flush with the worst of them.

What is a valve presetting?

It is a setting on the radiator valve (with a ring or a mark) that limits the maximum flow through that radiator. It is set during balancing, and changing it also changes how heat is shared around the house.
