Fitting new water pipes in an exposed run costs from €10 per metre, and a new cold, hot or waste outlet at a fixture from €35; pipes, fittings and chasing are extra. This page is about how modern multilayer pipes are joined and tested, and it leads on to the pages on risers, leaks and sewer pipes.

The pressed joint and its first test

In flats, multilayer pipes are joined with press fittings: the pipe is pushed into the fitting and the sleeve is crimped with a press tool. A fitting left pushed on but not pressed can hold water at low pressure and start leaking only later, when the pipes are already in the wall.

For its RadoPress system (sizes 16–32) the manufacturer Pipelife sets a separate step before the leak tightness test: the system is tested at 3 bar for 15 minutes. This low pressure is meant precisely to reveal unpressed joints. Only then comes the actual tightness test according to the maker’s instructions.

Pipelife, Plumbing Installation Guidelines (RadoPress system), checked on 25 November 2026. Another maker’s test procedure may differ.

A square cut and a calibrated pipe edge

The other thing a pressed run cannot do without is preparing the pipe end. Pipelife states that after a square cut the pipe is calibrated with a tool of the matching diameter. In one action it restores the inner diameter and chamfers the outer and inner edges, and the swarf is removed. An unprepared edge can damage the seal ring as soon as it is pushed in.

You cannot tell from the edge afterwards, because after pressing all joints look the same. The quality of a new run can be judged by whether every end was prepared and whether the pressure test was done before the pipes were covered.

PEX pipe: slack, supports and substances to keep away

Alongside multilayer pipes, PEX pipes are also used in flats. For example, the Wavin guide from the United States gives three installation points for its SuperPEX pipes that serve as general guidance:

Slack
the pipe is not pulled tight, because it expands and contracts with temperature, and a taut pipe loads the fittings needlessly.
Supports
they must not crush or cut into the pipe, must have no sharp edges and must let it move; a kinked pipe is not installed, and the damaged section is cut out.
Substances
the pipe must not touch thread sealing compounds, petroleum-based sealants, solvents or strong chlorine solutions.

If pipes are laid afresh throughout a flat, point 111 of LBN 221-15 covers new and altered buildings. It provides for air release at the highest points of the hot water system (possible through the higher taps too) and drainage at the lowest. In an old house this is a reference point, but it helps to decide where to leave a valve in the run.

Standard service life of multilayer pipe

Annex 2 to Cabinet Regulation No. 907 gives an average standard service life of 15–20 years for multilayer cold and hot water pipes. It is a figure for surveying and planning a building under normal operating conditions, not a deadline by which a pipe must be changed.

One practical point follows for a flat owner: if pipes are concealed in a wall or floor, the joints must be possible to find later. A photograph with a tape measure before finishing can help locate the run years later without opening up the whole wall.

An exposed run or pipes in the wall

The price from €10 per metre is for an exposed run, where the pipe is fixed to the wall or floor and stays visible. If pipes go into the wall or floor, chasing and finishing are added, and that is a separate job. Three things change the labour price: the length and bends of the run, the installation method and the type of pipe.

An exposed run is easier to inspect and repair, but it shows. A concealed run looks cleaner, but its joints cannot be reached, which is why the first pressure test described by Pipelife and a photo with a tape measure matter. A flat can also take a mixed option: pipes in the wall, with the distribution point or manifold behind a removable panel.

Pressing, testing and pipe service life

What is an unpressed joint?

It is a fitting with the pipe pushed in but the sleeve not crimped by the tool. In the Pipelife RadoPress system it is found by testing at 3 bar for 15 minutes before the tightness test.

Must multilayer pipes be changed after 20 years?

No, not automatically. The 15–20 years in Cabinet Regulation No. 907 are an average standard service life for planning, not a deadline by which the pipe must be removed.

When is work priced per metre and when per outlet?

Per metre covers the length of new pipe in an exposed run. Per outlet covers one branch to a fixture, such as a cold water supply to a new sink. One run can include both.
