Cabinet Regulation No. 907 also requires a visual inspection of the building’s pipe networks after storms, floods and heavy rain. In buildings nearer the Daugava, the cellar is worth looking at first on such an occasion. A defect inspection costs from €40.

Inspection after a downpour, storm or flood

Point 14 of the Regulation provides that the visual inspection of pipe networks is also carried out after storms, floods, heavy precipitation and other natural events that can cause damage. It also applies in the cases set out in standard LVS 1077:2023. So inspection is not tied to the calendar alone: a single heavy downpour can call for it.

According to the neighbourhoods portal, the narrower part of Ķengarags, from the Daugava to Latgales iela, lies 4–8 m above sea level. The larger part between that street and the railway lies at 8–11 m. The Regulation itself says what to look for in a cellar. Sub-point 11.6 requires checking walls, floors and ceilings for damp, mould or condensation, water pipes for condensation, and whether water is seeping into the cellar.

Condensation on a cold water pipe and a leak from it look alike in a cellar. If damp appears on the pipe only in warm, humid weather and the joints are dry, it is usually condensation. If one joint is wet or a puddle collects under it, that is a leak and is repaired as one.

Source: Cabinet Regulation No. 907, points 3, 3.1, 11.6, 13–15 and 19, likumi.lv, checked 25 November 2026.

Once a year, and sample checks in flats

Apart from natural events, an inspection is also due once a year. Point 3 of Cabinet Regulation No. 907 applies as worded by Cabinet Regulation No. 412 of 21 July 2026. It states that the technical condition of the building, its structure, equipment and pipe networks is established by visual inspection once a year. The fact of the inspection is entered in the building inspection register, which is part of the building file.

Risers and other parts of the shared networks also run through flats. For these, point 3.1 provides for a sample inspection once a year if the owner gives access; if not, this is recorded in the inspection register. In a flat where the riser is behind tiled boxing, it is also worth noting during the inspection whether the riser can be reached at all.

From inspection record to repair decision

  1. Point 13 of the Regulation requires a visual inspection of the heating, water supply and sewer systems in the common-use rooms.
  2. If the inspection finds damage listed in point 17, under point 15 the manager decides on unscheduled repair or prepares proposals for a decision by the community of flat owners.
  3. If the inspection establishes circumstances that may cause damage, point 19 requires the manager to take steps to prevent it.
  4. A plumber’s inspection supplies what the decision needs: where the fault is, what needs doing and what it would cost.

Another reason not to put work off is Article 29 of the Law on the Management of Residential Houses. It provides for a warning or a fine for failing to carry out a compulsory management action where this affects the public interest. Article 14, part 3 of that law also requires the manager to warn the building owner in writing of urgent measures needed to protect the building from ruin, collapse or destruction. Their expected cost must be calculated too. Part 4 requires owners to be informed in good time if one owner’s action or inaction affects others' interests, for example when the riser in a flat cannot be reached.

Water risers and soil stacks, one flat high

If the inspection shows that a riser needs replacing, the price list states the job for the height of one flat. Replacing a water riser section costs from €110: one length taken out and a new one put in, joined to the existing pipe above and below. Replacing a soil stack section costs from €100: sawing out the old length and inserting a new one with an adapter to the remaining pipe.

Neither sum includes materials or the building manager’s approval. For a water riser the sum depends on the state of the existing pipe, opening the pipe shaft and when water can be shut off in the building. For a soil stack it depends on the old pipe’s material, access to the shaft and the type of adapters. How the cost of new risers for a whole building is shared between owners is explained in Who pays for new risers.

Managers' questions about inspections

After heavy rain the cellar is damp, but nothing is dripping. Is an inspection still needed?

Point 14 of the Regulation ties the inspection to the event itself, namely heavy precipitation, a storm or flooding, not to whether something is already dripping. A damp cellar is a good reason to carry one out.

Can there be a penalty for an omitted compulsory management action?

Article 29 of the Law on the Management of Residential Houses provides for a warning or a fine for the building owner or manager. This applies if a compulsory management action is omitted and the public interest is affected.

The inspection found a leaking joint on a shared riser. Who decides what to do?

Under point 15 of Cabinet Regulation No. 907 the manager decides on unscheduled repair or prepares proposals for a decision by the community. The plumber can be requested by whoever has taken the decision.
