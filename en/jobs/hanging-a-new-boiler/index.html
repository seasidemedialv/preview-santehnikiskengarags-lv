Water heater installation - free room beside the casing for servicing

Before a water heater installation, it pays to measure the wall and also the space at the sides of the casing, because the heater will need to be reached for servicing later.

Labour starts from €70 for putting a storage heater up on a suitable wall and joining it to the water supply. The unit itself, valves, the power line and removal of the old appliance are charged separately.

Clear space on both sides of the casing

The manufacturer Electrolux requires, for its EWH Heatronic DryHeat series, a gap of at least 0.2 m from the walls to the heater casing on both sides for servicing. So this model cannot be squeezed into a niche between two cupboards or hung in a corner tight against a side wall.

The gap is not for looks. During servicing the tank is drained, the flange with the element is removed and the anode is checked, which needs room for hands and tools. A heater boxed in too tightly may have to come off the wall for servicing, and planning the space on installation day avoids that extra work. Other makers may ask for something different, so the space is planned from the manual of the actual model.

A small heater at the sink

For its 5-15 litre heaters (GT 5-15), placed at a kitchen or bathroom sink, the manufacturer Gorenje sets three requirements for the location:

Fixing
to the wall with screws at least 5 mm in diameter.
Room
protected from frost, so an unheated garage or shed will not do.
Distance
as close as possible to the point of use, so that you do not wait long for hot water through a long pipe.

Such a heater is small, yet the wall still has to be assessed. The mounting price changes with the volume and weight, the load-bearing capacity of the wall and how far away the pipes are.

The floor below and two hooks in the wall

In its storage heater manual the manufacturer TESY requires the room to have a waterproofed floor and a drain to the sewer. Without waterproofing, a protective tray with an outlet to the sewer is needed under the heater, and things that water could damage must not be kept below it. If you want the heater in a hallway or in a kitchen cupboard above a shelf, take this into account when choosing the spot.

Vertical TESY heaters need two hooks, at least 10 mm in diameter, fixed securely in the wall, with 220–310 mm between them depending on the model. The hooks are not in the kit, and which to use depends on the wall material.

Expansion vessel and a dripping safety valve

As water in the tank warms, its volume grows and the surplus has to go somewhere. Without an extra vessel it usually leaves through the safety valve, which may drip every time the heater warms up. Gorenje offers a solution: to stop the safety valve dripping, an expansion vessel of at least 5% of the heater volume is fitted on the heater inlet pipe.

Gorenje GT 5-15 manual and Electrolux EWH Heatronic DryHeat manual, checked on 25 November 2026. These are requirements for particular models; the expansion vessel is a separate part that the mounting price does not include.

The safety valve must not be plugged or removed to stop it dripping. The right way is to give the water room in a vessel or lead the valve drips into a drain.

The safety valve supplied in the box

TESY warns that other or old safety valves can damage the appliance. They are therefore removed, and the supplied valve is used, or a separately bought one if local rules require it. No shut-off fitting may sit between the combined non-return and safety valve and the heater: a valve there could cut the tank off from the valve that protects it.

If the valve drips are piped away, TESY requires the free end of the pipe to stay open to the air and not be submerged in water. Both pipe and valve must be protected from frost. The stop valve goes before the valve group, not between the valve and the tank.

TESY manual for electric storage water heaters, viewed on 25 November 2026. Requirements of other makers may differ.

A stop valve at the cold inlet

A heater usually gets its own stop valve at the cold water inlet. It lets you isolate the heater without turning off water to the whole flat, for example during cleaning or repair. A valve on the hot outlet is not compulsory, but it makes draining the tank easier.

Valves are not in the mounting price. Their choice depends on the heater volume, the pressure in the house pipes and whether a safety valve comes with the heater. A place to attach a hose or stand a bucket also helps when draining the tank.

The unit, valves and power line

The mounting labour from €70 covers hanging the heater and connecting it to the water pipes. The heater itself, valves, the power line and removal of the old appliance cost extra.

If the heater is moved elsewhere and pipes have to run to the new spot, the new pipes are charged at pipework rates. If the old heater still works but only heats slowly or makes noise, cleaning or repair can be considered first.

Wall, space and valve

Will a boiler fit in a narrow niche between cupboards?

It depends on the model. The Electrolux EWH Heatronic DryHeat series needs at least 0.2 m left on both sides of the casing for servicing. If the niche lacks that, choose a model whose manual allows it.

Why an expansion vessel if there is already a safety valve?

The valve protects the tank from excess pressure, but may release surplus water as it warms. An expansion vessel (at least 5% of the heater volume according to Gorenje) keeps this water in the system so the valve does not drip.

Can a small heater go in an unheated room?

For the small GT 5-15 models Gorenje requires a room protected from frost. Such a heater should not be left in an unheated shed or garage in winter.

What happens to the old boiler?

Removing the old appliance is not in the mounting price and is charged separately. It can be arranged together with mounting the new heater.
