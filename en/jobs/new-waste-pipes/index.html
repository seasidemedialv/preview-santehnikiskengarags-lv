Sewer pipe replacement - the gap in the socket for heat expansion

Sewer pipe replacement in a flat usually means the branch from the soil stack to one fixture: a bath, sink, toilet or washing machine.

Such a job costs from €30 per connection and includes setting the fall; pipes, bends and floor reinstatement are extra. Old cast iron or grey plastic is usually replaced with a new plastic system, which has its own installation rules.

Why the pipe is not pushed all the way home

For its HT system the manufacturer Ostendorf states that after the pipe has been pushed fully into the socket, it must be pulled back 10 mm. This gap is left so that the pipe can lengthen with heat. When hot water from a bath, dishwasher or washing machine runs down the drain, the plastic stretches slightly and the pipe needs somewhere to slide.

If the pipe is jammed against the bottom of the socket, the stretching pushes on the joint and the nearby clips. Over time this can shift the seal ring or bow a longer stretch, and the joint may then start seeping where it cannot be seen from the floor. So when old pipe is replaced, the correct diameter matters, and so does the way each joint is put together.

Clip spacing, lying and upright

The new pipe has to be held to the wall or floor so that it does not sag or lose its fall. Ostendorf gives these recommended clip spacings for its HT system:

Pipe Lying (horizontal) Upright (vertical)
DN 110, toilet and stack connection 1.1 m 2.0 m
DN 50, sink, bath, machine 0.5 m 1.5 m

Figures from another maker may differ, and the instructions for the system actually being installed prevail. Old cast iron brackets suit new plastic only if they match the pipe size and sit at the required spacing.

Lubricant on the pipe end, not on the ring

The second Ostendorf instruction concerns the assembly itself. Lubricant is spread thinly only on the chamfer of the pipe end, and there must be none on the seal ring at the moment of pushing in. A greased ring can move out of its groove during insertion, so the joint may look right without being watertight.

For its AS+ system the manufacturer Wavin also describes what happens before lubricating: the pipe is cut square, at 90° to the axis. Burrs are removed and a chamfer about 5 mm long at 15° is ground on the end to be inserted. Before assembly the seal is checked to be intact and seated properly in the socket. Socket, seal and pipe end are cleaned, and the insertion depth is marked on the pipe. The mark shows whether the pipe has gone in as far as needed.

Chamfer
the bevelled pipe edge that lets it slide into the ring without damaging it.
Seal ring
the rubber ring inside the socket that seals the joint.
Fall
the slope of the pipe towards the stack, to be kept along the whole branch.

A fall without sags, and the bends

A waste pipe must drop evenly towards the stack along its full length. If there is a sag in the middle, water stands in it, and over time grease and dirt settle there. This is why branch length and clip positions go together: a pipe that droops between clips creates a sag of its own.

Every bend slows the flow a little and is a place where dirt can catch. The number of bends also changes the price, and a route with fewer of them is simpler to install and later to clean. If the branch cannot be laid with the required fall, for instance because the fixture is too far from the stack, that must be known before the work, not afterwards.

A pipe in the floor, in concrete or boxed in

Point 239.2 of LBN 221-15 allows sewer pipes to be concealed under the floor, behind cladding, in boxing or in pipe shafts, with hatches provided opposite the access openings. Boxing in a new branch in the bathroom is allowed if the cleaning opening can be reached.

If a pipe is cast into concrete, Wavin requires the sockets to be sealed with adhesive tape, open ends to be closed and the pipe to be firmly fixed. The concrete vibrator must not touch the pipe directly. Point 236 of LBN 221-15 forbids right-angle crosses in the horizontal plane: a branch joins at an oblique angle so that the flows do not run into each other.

LBN 221-15 is a design building standard for new and altered buildings; for a flat in an old block it is a point of reference. The Wavin AS+ instructions apply to that system. Viewed on 25 November 2026.

The branch from stack to fixture

The price from €30 is for relaying one waste branch from the soil stack to one fixture and setting its fall. The sum changes with branch length, the number of bends and whether the pipe runs in the floor. If the old waste pipe is cast into concrete, freeing it and reinstating the floor are a separate job charged on top.

If a soil stack section has to be changed at the same time, that is a different job with its own price, covered on the riser replacement page. A blockage in an old pipe does not yet mean it must be replaced: drain cleaning may help first.

Plastic, clips and branches

Does a plastic waste pipe expand only with very hot water?

It expands with any warmer water, only more with hot water. That is why Ostendorf requires a 10 mm gap in the socket at every joint, not only where a dishwasher is connected.

Can the old wall clips be used for the new pipe?

Yes, if they fit the size and sit at the right spacing. For a lying DN 50 pipe in the Ostendorf HT system a support every 0.5 m is recommended; another maker’s figures may differ.

A room has a sink and a washing machine: how many connections is that?

The price is counted per branch from the stack to one fixture. If each appliance gets its own branch, that is two connections; on a shared one, the branch length and bends change the price.

Can a concreted-in waste pipe be replaced without opening the floor?

Usually not. A pipe running in the floor is one of the price factors, and opening and reinstating the floor are not part of this price.
