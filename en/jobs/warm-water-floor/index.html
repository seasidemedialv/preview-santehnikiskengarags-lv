Water underfloor heating installation - the first warm-up after the screed

Water underfloor heating installation does not end on the day the pipes are laid and the screed is poured: the first warm-up is a separate stage that cannot be rushed.

Laying and fixing the pipes is charged per square metre. The price is set by the floor area and number of loops, the readiness of the base and insulation, the manifold position and the heat source. There is no single starting price, because every house has a different floor.

Temperature in stages after the screed

For example, the protocol of one underfloor heating supplier in the United Kingdom has the first warm-up start slowly, with the temperature raised step by step over days. The makers of your floor covering and system still have the main say about your floor, so this is an example, not a standard:

Stage Flow water temperature Duration
Start 25 °C 72 hours
Raising the temperature about 5 °C a day, no higher than 55 °C until the maximum temperature
Holding maximum temperature at least 4 days

The slow rise is for the sake of the new screed: heated with hot water straight away, it can crack. A maker of floor coverings such as tiles or parquet may set its own procedure, which must be followed.

An example from a UK supplier’s pressure test and warm-up protocol (viewed on 25 November 2026). It is not a Latvian requirement, and the floor covering maker’s directions matter more.

Base, insulation and loops

Before the pipes go down the floor must be ready: a level base and insulation to fix the pipes onto are needed. How ready these are is one of the price factors, because pipes cannot be laid without a prepared base.

A loop is a single pipe that leaves the manifold, winds across the room and returns. A large room has several loops, each connected to the manifold separately. More loops and a larger area mean more work.

The loop is laid with bends, and the pipe must not be kinked while bending. For example, Danfoss gives for its 16 mm FH multilayer pipe a minimum bending radius of 80 mm with a hand bender and 64 mm with a bending spring.

Before the screed is poured the circuits are pressure tested. The same UK protocol sets 4–6 bar for at least 20 minutes for composite manifolds, using water or air. Pouring the screed and the floor covering are not included in the pipe laying work.

A wooden floor over the heating

The floor covering may have stricter requirements than the pipes. For example, the wood floor maker Kährs allows its floor to be laid over heating only after all tests of the heating system are complete. Kährs requires an approved type of vapour barrier over a heated subfloor and specifies an ageing-resistant polyethylene film 0.2 mm thick. Kährs also mentions:

Room humidity
before, during and after laying, relative air humidity must be 30-60%.
Temperature
material, subfloor and air at least 15 °C while laying.
Sensitivity
Kährs warns that a wooden floor over heating is more sensitive to humidity, since the moisture in the wood varies over a wider range there.

A private house, a refit or a flat

Water underfloor heating needs a heat source and a place for the manifold, so it fits more easily into a private house with its own boiler or a house being rebuilt. Private houses around Ķengarags are found, for example, in Rumbula and Dārziņi. In a block of flats on district heating, connecting a floor to the building system usually has to be agreed with the building manager.

The floor after the screed and the pipes inside

Why can full heat not be switched on at the first warm-up?

New screed holds moisture and may crack if heated sharply. For example, the protocol of a UK supplier holds 25 °C for 72 hours and then raises the temperature by about 5 °C a day.

Are the underfloor pipes pressure tested before the screed?

Yes, that is a usual step. For example, the same UK protocol sets 4–6 bar for at least 20 minutes for composite manifolds, using water or air.

Is pouring the screed in the price?

No. The price per square metre covers laying and fixing the pipes. The base, insulation and screed are separate jobs; the readiness of the base and insulation affects the price.

What is the standard service life of floor heating with polymer pipes?

Annex 2 to Cabinet Regulation No. 907 gives 40 years as the average standard service life of a floor heating system with polymer pipes in houses of durability groups I-V. It is an average for planning a building, not the date a floor must be opened.
