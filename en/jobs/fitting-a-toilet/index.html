Toilet installation - floor screws and what lies beneath them

Toilet installation starts with a few screws in the floor, and each is a hole that may hit a pipe or cable and break the waterproofing.

Replacing a floor pan (unscrewing the old one, fixing the new one, joining water and waste) costs from €50; pan, pan connector, hose and removal are extra. Putting up a wall-hung pan on a hidden frame is a different job from €85, without frame, pan, boarding or tiles.

Drilling depth in the floor

For example, Swedish installation rules attached to a Geberit instruction provide that the floor under a floor-standing toilet must allow 60 mm for drilling and screwing. Pipes and electric cables under the fixing spot may only lie deeper than 60 mm. This is not a Latvian requirement, but it shows well where the risk hides.

In a bathroom with underfloor heating or with floor pipes running to a basin or washing machine, you need to know where they are before drilling. If there is no plan of the runs, old photographs or the position of the manifold, for example, can help to work out where the pipes are. The price of the work also depends on the floor base.

Swedish industry installation rules in an annex to a Geberit instruction, checked 25 November 2026. It is a foreign example, not a Latvian standard.

Mark the floor once the pan stands in place

For example, a guide by the British retailer PlumbNation for a close-coupled toilet suggests an order that prevents crooked screws. First level the pan and push it fully to the wall, and only then mark the floor at the fixing holes. A little grease goes on the seal of the waste connector, and the pan is pushed in until the connector sits fully on the sewer.

The same guide warns repeatedly against overtightening either the cistern screws to the pan or the pan fixings to the floor: porcelain cracks. The water supply is joined to the cistern with a 1/2 inch flat-faced connector, and a washer is a must.

For new and rebuilt buildings, point 118.9 of LBN 221-15 provides for a stop fitting on the supply to a flushing cistern. Point 121 allows it to be omitted if the flat’s branch pipe has one. In an old building a small valve at the cistern later lets you change the mechanism without shutting off water to the whole flat.

Screw holes and waterproofing

The same Swedish rules require screw fixings to be sealed against the floor waterproofing with a compound that resists water, mould and ageing. A hole in a tile is also a hole in the waterproofing below, and if it is not sealed, water on the floor can run down along the screw.

In a block of flats the neighbour’s ceiling is below. So sealing the screw is a small but important part of the work, done before the pan is set in place.

The water supply connector

The maker Fluidmaster advises that when changing a fill valve you also check the water supply connector and replace it if it is worn or more than 5 years old. This is useful when installing a new toilet too: if the supply hose is older, it can be changed along with the toilet while the water is off.

The first flush after fitting

After installation the toilet is flushed several times and the joints are inspected: the pan connector between pan and waste, and the supply hose at the cistern. A dry paper towel left under a joint shows even a small drip that the eye may miss.

The pan connector is a rubber or plastic coupling between the pan outlet and the waste pipe. If the new pan’s outlet is at a different position or height from the old one, a different connector or bend is needed. That is one reason the outlet direction changes the price.

A wall-hung pan on a frame

Putting it up on a hidden frame, from €85, includes securing the frame, joining the cistern to water and waste and hanging the pan. The frame, pan, boards and tiling are not in this sum. The price changes with the wall type, the waste height and the distance to the soil stack.

Pan, floor and supply

Can the floor be drilled if underfloor heating pipes run under the toilet?

Only where there are no pipes. The Swedish rules provide that under a floor pan, pipes and cables must lie deeper than 60 mm. If nobody knows where the pipes run, that has to be established before drilling.

Why seal the screw holes if the pan stands on tiles?

A hole in a tile is also a hole in the waterproofing. The Swedish rules require it to be sealed with a water-resistant, mould-resistant compound, so that water from the floor does not run down along the screw.

How old does a supply hose have to be to change it with the toilet?

Fluidmaster advises replacing the connector if it is worn or more than 5 years old. The hose is a separate part added to the labour price.

What is a pan connector, and is it changed with the toilet?

It is the coupling between the pan outlet and the waste pipe. A new pan usually gets a new connector, because the old one may have hardened or be a different size. The connector is a part added to the labour price.
