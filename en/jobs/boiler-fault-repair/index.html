Water heater repair - a tap that drips while the water warms

Not every dripping tap on a small heater means that water heater repair is needed: on an open-outlet (unpressurised) connection the tap is meant to behave this way during heating.

The tap that drips during heating

Small heaters under or over a sink are often fitted on an open, or unpressurised, connection. Here the tank is linked to the open air through the tap spout, and as the water warms, its extra volume escapes through the tap. The manufacturer Gorenje says this plainly about its GT 5-15 series. On an open connection the tap drips during heating, and closing it more tightly cannot stop the dripping.

Gorenje also warns that forcing it shut can only damage the tap. If the tap on an open connection drips during heating but stops once the water is hot, that is normal. A tap that also drips when the heater is not heating, or water seeping from the casing or joints, is already a reason for an inspection.

Gorenje GT 5-15 user manual, checked on 25 November 2026. An open-outlet connection needs a mixer tap designed for it; an ordinary pressure tap is not used with such a heater.

Heating element, thermostat and spare parts

The replaceable parts in a storage heater are the heating element, the thermostat, the seal under the flange and the anode. The repair price depends on three things, usually established by looking at the heater itself:

Which part has failed
changing a thermostat and changing an element take different amounts of work, because the tank must be drained for the element.
Whether the spare part can be bought
for some models the maker’s parts are no longer sold, which makes repair harder or no longer possible.
Tank age and corrosion
if the flange seat or the tank has rusted, a new seal may no longer hold.

If the tank holds a lot of limescale, the element change can be combined with cleaning. Descaling the tank is a separate job, described on the page about water heaters.

Whistling, limescale and a blinking lamp

The manufacturer TESY explains in its storage heater manual that a whistle during heating, sounding like boiling water, is not a fault in itself. Over time limescale makes it louder, and removing the noise takes cleaning the heater. Limescale makes the element surface run hotter and the thermostat switch on and off more often. The overheating protection may trip even though there is no real overheating.

On TESY models with an electronic panel the number of blinks shows the error. For example, five blinks mean the element has been switched on without water, and the manual then says to contact the maker’s authorised service. Write down the blink count before switching the heater off.

Draining the tank before the flange comes off

The tank has to be drained to change the element or the seal. TESY describes the order: first the power is disconnected, then the water supply to the heater is shut off, the hot side of the mixer tap is opened and the drain valve is opened. If there is no drain valve, the water is let out through the inlet pipe. Even then a few more litres run out of the tank when the flange is removed. So place a bucket under the heater and move away anything water could damage.

Source: TESY manual for electric storage water heaters (viewed on 25 November 2026). Error codes and the procedure differ on other models.

Spare parts matched by the rating plate

Finding the maker’s spare part takes the heater model and, if possible, the serial number. They are usually printed on the rating plate on the heater casing. One maker’s elements and thermostats differ between models in power, length and fixing, so a part cannot be chosen by looks alone.

If the plate has come off, or the heater is from a maker that no longer sells parts, the repair may drag on or be impossible. This is exactly why the availability of spare parts is one of the factors behind the repair price.

A consumer's claim about a service

Under the Consumer Rights Protection Law a consumer can bring a claim about a non-conforming service within two years of receiving the service. The notice of non-conformity must be submitted at least within two months from the day the non-conformity was discovered. This applies to services in general, not just plumbing.

In practice this means that if the same fault returns after a repair, it is worth reporting it in writing and soon, without waiting. Keep the proof of payment and note the date the fault reappeared.

Heater, tap and parts

Can an ordinary mixer tap go on an open-outlet heater?

No. An open connection needs a tap designed for it, because the tank is open to the air through the spout. An ordinary pressure tap can damage such a heater.

Is it possible to change the element in an old boiler?

Yes, if the maker’s part is still sold and the flange seat has not rusted. If the tank itself is corroded, a new part may not last long, and a new heater is then worth considering.

Where is the heater model shown, for finding a part?

On the rating plate, which is usually on the heater casing. If you photograph it, the model and number will be to hand when the part is searched for.

How soon must a fault that returns after repair be reported?

The Consumer Rights Protection Law provides for a notice at least within two months from the day you discovered the non-conformity, and a claim within two years of receiving the service.

Does the defect inspection include the repair?

No. At the inspection the plumber comes, looks at the faulty assembly and says what needs doing and what it would cost. The repair itself and parts are not part of the inspection sum.
