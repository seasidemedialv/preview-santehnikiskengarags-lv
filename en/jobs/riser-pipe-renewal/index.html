Riser pipe replacement in a flat - the old stub in the slab between floors

Changing a hot or cold water riser section costs from €110 and a soil stack section from €100; materials and the building manager’s approval are not in these sums. Before the work you must decide exactly where the new pipe will end: at the ceiling or inside the floor slab.

The piece in the slab that stays old

In a 2019 explanation on LV portāls, the head of the property managers' association warns that flat renovations often replace pipes only up to the slab between floors. An old piece then remains in the concrete, where nobody sees it. If exactly that bursts, it is a part of the common property, for which the community of flat owners and the building manager answer, not your new pipe.

In practice this means a choice. The joint can be left close to the ceiling and floor, where the pipe leaves the concrete. Or you can try to extend the new section deeper, to where the neighbour’s pipe begins. The second option needs access from the neighbour’s side and depends on how sound the old metal at the slab is. Make this choice while the boxing is still open, because once finished the old piece is hidden for years.

LV portāls, explanation on a flooded flat and compensation for losses (2019), checked on 25 November 2026. This is practical advice, not a legal rule.

Niche, boxing and a removable panel

In the large-panel blocks of Ķengarags, including the widespread 467A series buildings with vertical loggia bands, risers in a flat are sometimes hidden by boxing or a niche wall. In LV portāls (2017) the head of the managers' association explains that providing access is more than opening the door. A covered riser has to be reachable, and for a niche he recommends an easily removable decorative panel.

Boxing without an opening
it has to be broken open to fit the new section, and restoring it is finishing work, not part of the riser price.
Removable panel
gives later access to joints and stop valves without chiselling.
Opening the pipe shaft
one of the factors that changes the labour price, since time also goes on getting to the pipe at all.

Circulation pipe and the owners' consent

In houses with hot water circulation a second vertical pipe runs beside the hot riser: the hot water circulation pipe. A reply from SIA “Rīgas namu pārvaldnieks” on LV portāls (2022) explains one case: work in a flat that affects common property, such as this circulation pipe. Such work needs the consent of the owners' community under Article 11, part 3 of the Apartment Property Law. It is a manager’s reply that does not bind those who apply the law, but it shows clearly where approval begins.

If the circulation pipe is as old as the riser, it may be worth changing both at once while the water is already off. The price from €110 does not cover the manager’s approvals, so start arranging those first.

Fixings and insulation for the new section

When a piece is cut out of the old pipe and a new one set in with repair sockets, the joint must not move. For its AS+ drainage system the manufacturer Wavin states: fixed brackets are installed at both ends of the existing pipe, or a tension-resistant clamp goes on each repair socket.

Expansion
point 113 of LBN 221-15 requires thermal expansion to be considered in pipeline design, with compensators where needed; for a hot riser that means room for the pipe to move.
Condensation
point 108 of the same building standard provides for insulation against condensation for pipes in shafts and ducts; otherwise a cold riser in a closed niche may sweat.
Noise
in buildings over three storeys the Wavin AS+ system provides for a 250 mm noise damping section between soil stack and lying drain, for example two 45° bends and a piece of pipe.

LBN 221-15 is a building standard for designing new and altered buildings; in a single-flat repair in an old house it is a reference point, not a duty in every case. Wavin AS+ requirements apply to that system; for another, see its manual. Viewed on 25 November 2026.

If the building later replaces all risers

Sometimes the risers in a flat are already new, but a few years later the building decides to replace them all. The association’s reply on LV portāls (2022) advises agreeing with the works manager to include the existing sections in the project. If the project allows no such option, the owner must not obstruct repair of common property, even if their own pipes are new.

If you change a section now, note down the pipe material, diameter and joint locations, and keep the receipts. In a later building-wide project this can help the works manager decide whether your section can stay. The same goes for the soil stack. A plastic pipe set in place of old cast iron, with a transition to the remaining part, should be described just as clearly, because the transition type also affects the price.

Section, slab and permissions

What does “one flat high” mean in the price?

It is the section from your floor to your ceiling, joined to the old pipes above and below it. The piece in the slab concrete is not included. If the section must go further, the price changes with the state of the existing pipe and whether the neighbour’s side can be reached.

What is the hot water circulation pipe, and why does changing it need the owners' consent?

It is the pipe that carries hot water back round so that you need not wait for it at the tap. It serves the whole building, so it is common property. SIA “Rīgas namu pārvaldnieks” states on LV portāls (2022) that work affecting it needs the owners' consent under Article 11, part 3 of the Apartment Property Law.

Why not leave the joint to the old pipe inside the slab concrete?

A joint in concrete cannot be seen or reached. If it starts seeping, water usually shows only on the neighbour’s ceiling or wall, and the slab must be chiselled open to get at it. So the joint is left where it can be inspected, and the niche needs a removable panel.

Who decides whether the new section goes through the slab?

The plumber judges whether it is technically possible and what it requires from the neighbour’s side. As the piece in the slab is part of the common property, work on it has to be agreed with the building manager, unless the house documents say otherwise.
