Sink installation - screws that must not be overtightened

During sink installation a porcelain wall basin can crack while it is still being mounted if its screws are tightened too hard.

Fixing the basin in a prepared spot and joining it to the existing drain with a trap costs from €40. The basin itself, the trap, the mixer tap and moving pipes are charged separately.

Tightening torque for a wall basin

The maker Laufen states two things in the assembly instructions for its Ino wall basin. Use all the fixing holes, and do not exceed a tightening torque of 5 Nm. 5 Nm is a small force, easy to exceed with an ordinary spanner.

All the holes are needed so that the load spreads evenly. If only two screws out of four hold the basin, they carry the whole weight and also the pressure when someone leans on it. An overtightened screw can crack the porcelain right at the fixing.

Laufen Ino wall basin assembly instructions, checked 25 November 2026. The maker may give a different torque for another model; follow its instructions.

A heavy basin on brackets

Requirements are stricter for large stone or ceramic basins on brackets. For example, for heavy wall-mounted basins the maker TAILOR-MADE requires a structural specialist to check the wall or load-bearing structure, and safe fitting takes two people. Brackets on the wall are checked with a spirit level, screws are tightened firmly but not overtightened, and the basin is levelled precisely with the brackets' adjusting screws.

Worktop first, basin afterwards

If the basin stands on a worktop or vanity top, the maker Oltens gives this order for its countertop basin: first fit and level the worktop, and protect the openings drilled in it with silicone. Only then put the basin on.

A stainless steel kitchen sink

Annex 2 of Cabinet Regulation No. 907 gives an average standard service life of 40 years for stainless steel washing-up sinks and kitchen sinks. The Regulation cites it as an average for planning a building’s repairs, and it does not mean the sink must be replaced afterwards.

In the kitchen a dishwasher can also be connected at the sink trap, and the trap then needs a spur. Connecting the machine has its own page on washing machine and dishwasher hook-up.

Trap height and the waste opening

Sink installation includes joining the basin to the existing drain with a trap, but the trap itself is a separate purchase. The trap height is adjusted so that its outlet meets the waste opening in the wall or floor without strain. If a trap has been forced together, the joint may start to seep in time.

The nuts on a plastic trap are usually tightened by hand, and after assembly the basin is filled and emptied while the joints are watched. A dry paper towel under the trap shows if anything is dripping.

The waste fitting goes in before the trap. For example, Victorian Plumbing instructions note that a basin with an overflow needs a slotted waste, and one without needs an unslotted waste. The wrong one either shuts off the overflow or leaves a gap. A thin bead of good silicone goes round the upper and lower rim of the waste hole, and the excess is wiped off.

When the old outlets do not line up

The price from €40 applies to a basin whose waste and water supplies are already in place. If the new basin has a different width or height and the outlets do not match it, moving the pipes is charged separately. The wall or worktop material is a third factor: fixings differ for concrete, brick and plasterboard.

Basin, worktop and fixings

Why must basin screws not be tightened as far as they go?

Porcelain is hard but brittle, and an overtightened screw can crack it. For its Ino basin, Laufen says not to exceed 5 Nm and to use all the fixing holes.

Can a countertop basin go on before the top is levelled?

Oltens says to fit and level the worktop first and only then put the basin on. On an uneven surface the basin does not sit evenly.

Why are worktop openings protected with silicone?

Oltens advises protecting the openings drilled in the top with silicone. An unprotected wood or chipboard edge soaks up water and can swell.

What may a new ceramic basin be cleaned with?

For example, for the care of sanitary ceramics Ifö advises against strong acids such as hydrochloric and sulphuric acid, strong alkalis such as caustic soda, steel wool and hard abrasives.
