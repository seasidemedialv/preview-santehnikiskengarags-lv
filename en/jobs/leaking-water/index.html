Water leak in a flat - the first suspect is under the sink

A water leak in a flat is worth tracing from under the sink first. In a 2019 explanation on LV portāls, the head of the building managers' association states that, in his experience, the most common cause of leaks is the flexible water pipes at the sink.

The flexible hose between tap and valve

The same explanation gives the reason: these hoses are not meant for very long use and are often badly connected. A hose joins the mixer tap to the valve on the pipe, and a similar hose also feeds the toilet cistern and the washing machine.

Open the cabinet under the sink and look at both nuts, the hose sheath itself and the cabinet floor. A damp nut, a rust streak on the braid, a bulging sheath or a stain on the base panel can show that the water comes from here. If only the joint is damp, resealing it is usually enough; if the sheath itself is damaged, the hose is replaced.

A bucket of water a week from one tap

A slow drip seems a trifle, but, for example, the Queensland government in Australia notes that one slowly dripping tap can waste more than a bucket of water a week. If a tap drips into a basin, the water at least goes down the drain. If a joint in a cabinet or behind a bath panel drips the same way, the same amount stays in the home.

In a block of flats this water has few places to stay. It soaks into a cabinet panel, gets under the floor covering or passes through the slab to the neighbour below, who notices it as a stain on the ceiling. So even a few drops a minute under the sink are a reason to check the joint before a stain appears in another flat.

After a leak, if your home has a policy

It is not only the policy terms that require action: the Insurance Contract Law does too. Article 27, part 1 states that the event must be reported immediately, all possible reasonable steps taken as soon as possible to limit the loss, and the instructions received followed. If this is not done through slight negligence, Article 29 allows the payment to be reduced, but by no more than 50 per cent. For malicious intent or gross negligence, payment may be refused.

Evidence is no less important, and the Latvian Insurers' Association (2026) advises photographing the spot and the damage and keeping damaged parts such as a burst hose. It also advises against unagreed repairs that could obscure the cause. Stopping the water need not wait, though: closing a valve and stopping the leak is one of the reasonable steps the law requires.

A damp patch in a wall or floor with no pipes in sight

If there is a stain on a wall, floor covering or ceiling but the visible pipes and hoses are dry, the leaking spot is found section by section. This job has no single starting price, and the sum depends on:

  • whether the damp comes from the wall, the floor or the neighbours' side;
  • how many sections need a pressure test;
  • whether finishes such as tiles or panelling must be opened.

If a hidden section means a pipe in a private house has to be replaced and the water cut off for longer, also observe point 68 of Riga binding regulations No. 17. It applies when a service user’s planned repairs need the supply interrupted and this affects other users. They are warned no later than three days ahead, and the service provider is informed in writing no later than five days ahead. This applies to planned work, not to an emergency.

If water comes from above and the pipes in your flat are dry, see the flooded flat page. If water is running right now and cannot be stopped, request an urgent visit.

On hoses and hidden spots

The hose under the sink looks sound. Is it still worth replacing?

If the nuts are dry and the sheath undamaged, there is usually no urgent need. The reason given in the explanation is that hoses are not meant for very long use, so when changing a mixer tap it is worth changing them with it.

If the policy does not pay for corrosion, what does that mean for a pipe that has seeped for months?

For example, BTA’s named-perils terms (No. 3F-3) do not pay for losses caused by long, gradual processes such as natural wear, corrosion or limescale. A slow leak from an old pipe can be exactly such a case, so it is better fixed than left. Your policy’s terms show what it covers.

Can a hidden leak be found without opening tiles?

Sometimes: by pressure testing the sections, it can be seen in which section water is being lost. Whether finishes still have to be opened is one of the things that change the tracing sum.
