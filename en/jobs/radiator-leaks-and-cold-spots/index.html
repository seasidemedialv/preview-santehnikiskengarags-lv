Radiator repair - a fault that surfaces in mid heating season

Radiator repair in the heating season is not just your flat’s affair, because in a block of flats the water in the radiator comes from the shared system.

If a radiator leaks or stays cold, the price depends on whether the fault is in the valve, the thermostat or a joint, whether water has to be drained and whether the part is available. To find out what needs doing, the radiator has to be looked at on site; a home visit with an inspection costs from €40, while the repair and parts are extra.

Unscheduled repair while the heating is on

Cabinet Regulation No. 907 on the survey and repair of residential houses sets out when a house gets an unscheduled repair, meaning one outside the plan. Sub-point 17.12 of the regulation lists damage to the heating system during the heating season as a reason for it. So a leaking radiator in January is not a job to leave until summer.

Source: sub-point 17.12 of Cabinet Regulation No. 907 (likumi.lv, viewed on 25 November 2026). In a block of flats such a repair is usually organised by the building manager, unless the house documents say otherwise.

In practice this means two things. First, if the damage affects the building’s part of the heating, the manager needs to know, because removing a radiator or shutting a riser usually needs their consent. Second, if the fault is only in the radiator valve or a joint, it may be possible to fix it without shutting off the whole building. That can only be judged at the radiator itself.

A valve pin stuck over summer

Not every cold radiator is broken. For example, a Finnish advice article explains that a cold radiator is often caused by the valve pin behind the thermostatic head. This small metal pin sticks in summer, when the head stays closed for a long time. To free it, the article suggests pressing it or lightly tapping the side of the valve. It is a practical tip, not a standard, and if the pin does not move or the valve starts dripping, call the plumber.

If the pin moves but the radiator still stays cold, the fault may be air, a blockage or the building system. There is more on this on the page about a radiator not heating.

Leaks, draining the water and parts

When the water has to come out

If a radiator joint leaks or the valve has to be replaced entirely, water usually has to be drained from the radiator or riser. In a block of flats this is agreed with the building manager, and the timing and price of the work depend on it.

When the part cannot be bought

Spare parts may no longer exist for older valves and thermostats. The repair may then mean replacing the whole valve, and installing a thermostatic valve becomes a separate job.

Bleeding with the supplied plug

For its panel radiators the manufacturer Kermi permits bleeding only through the bleed plug supplied in the kit, which must not be swapped for another shop-bought one. Kermi also explains why bleeding continues for a moment after water starts flowing: the plug frees the radiator panels one after another. Hot water can scald, so hold a container or cloth under the plug and protect your hand.

Kermi advises bleeding a radiator after it is put into use and after longer breaks in operation, with an ordinary radiator key. In a block of flats on central heating, air in a radiator may also be a matter of the building system, and the manager then needs to know about it.

Valve body and thermostatic head

A thermostatic valve has two parts. The body is screwed into the pipe and is permanently full of water. The head sits on top and, as the room temperature changes, presses the valve pin, opening or closing the flow. The head can come off without draining water, but changing the body requires the radiator to be isolated and drained.

That makes installing a thermostatic valve in place of an old plain one a bigger job than changing a head. In a block of flats it is usually agreed with the building manager, because the valve body is linked to the building heating system.

For example, the instructions for a HUMMEL Design thermostatic head say to turn the head to position 5, fully open, before fitting, and only then to tighten it with the M 30 x 1.5 nut. The head should not be mounted pointing upwards, as it then does not regulate accurately. The procedure may differ for other heads.

Cleaning the radiator surface

If you want to clean the dust off after a repair, the manufacturer Purmo states that the radiator should be cleaned with a solution of household detergent, without products containing ammonia or abrasives.

The radiator during the season

What does unscheduled repair mean?

It is repair done outside the building plan once damage has been found. Sub-point 17.12 of Cabinet Regulation No. 907 names damage to the heating system during the heating season as a reason for it.

What may a radiator be washed with?

Purmo advises a solution of household detergent. The maker advises against ammonia and abrasive products.

How does a thermostatic head differ from the valve body?

The head is a regulator that can be taken off by hand, while the body is part of the pipe and full of water. Changing the head needs no draining; changing the body does.

The valve pin will not move. Can it be freed with a tool?

A Finnish advice article suggests pressing the pin or lightly tapping the side of the valve. Do not force it with a tool: if the pin stays stuck or the valve starts dripping, it is a job for a plumber.
