Fixtures, parts and materials are not in the sum. Jobs with a figure also have an upper sum, which shows how far the price usually goes in a harder case.

What the upper sum beside a starting price tells you

The starting price matches the simplest case: the joint or fitting is visible, accessible and exactly as the price list describes it. The upper sum shows where the price usually stops if the job is harder. For example, the price for sealing a seeping joint covers one visible spot within arm’s reach. On an old steel pipe, behind boxing, or where the riser has to be shut first, the price moves towards the upper sum.

These circumstances differ from job to job. For a tap they are its construction, rust and whether it is in the kitchen, bathroom or shower. For a riser section they are the state of the old pipe, opening the pipe shaft and when water can be shut off in the building.

What no price here includes

All sums are for the plumber’s labour. The fixture itself (tap, toilet, water heater, radiator), parts, pipes and fittings, chasing channels into a wall, restoring finishes and taking away the old fixture are separate. For work on common property, such as risers, the sum also leaves out the building manager’s approval.

Questions about the sums

Is a job on a higher floor more expensive?

The floor is not among the things that change the sum. Access to the fitting itself does, for example when the riser is behind tiled boxing or in a shaft that has to be opened.

Why does descaling a water heater start higher than mounting one?

Descaling means draining the tank, washing out the deposits, cleaning the heating element and looking at the anode. Mounting is hanging the heater on a suitable wall and joining it to the water pipes, without opening the tank.

Can these prices be used to plan new risers for the whole building?

No. The riser prices are for one section the height of one flat. A project for the whole building needs its own estimate, and how that sum is shared between owners is explained in Who pays for new risers.
